# mini-cauldron: TBL's precalc sample synth (Jizz / Stash, 1997)

Status: **reverse-engineered and verified bit-exact** (2026-10-29).
The Python references in `ref/` reproduce all 32 generated samples of Jizz and
all 53 generated samples of Stash byte for byte. They were checked against the
original x86 code running under emulation, and the emulated `jizz.xm` is
byte-identical to the official scene.org rip.
Reproduce: `tools/run_all.sh` (≈ 2 min). Golden hashes: `ref/golden_sha256.json`.

This document is the implementation spec. Read it with the reference code
open; every rule here is exercised by the references.

---

## 1. What the music system is

Jizz (Wired '97, 1st, PC 64k) and Stash (The Party '97, 1st, PC 64k) by The
Black Lotus play **FastTracker 2 XM modules** (Jizz: 940 876 bytes, Stash: two
XMs of 899 710 and 1 006 301 bytes). The XMs never exist in the 64 KB file:

1. **XM template.** Patterns, order list, instrument and sample headers and a
   few small stored samples (≈ 14 KB of 8-bit PCM: vocals and the drum kit).
   The sample-data areas of every synthesized sample are left zero. This is
   in the executable and compresses to ≈ 15 KB (xz -9e).
2. **mini-cauldron.** At startup, during the precalc progress bar, a tiny synth
   renders every synthesized sample into the template:
   - v1 (Jizz) is ≈ 1.4 KB of x87 code plus 12 patch records of 123 bytes.
   - v2 (Stash) is ≈ 2 KB plus 26 records of 103 bytes.

   The XM names it: *"all samples generated by mini-cauldron coded by probe"*.
   v1 samples are named `ProbeNV.smp` and v2 samples `JaceNV.smp`.
3. **Playback.** A normal XM replayer plays the module. On DOS that was the
   GUS driver; iXalance/Win32/Linux hands the XM to MIDAS (`fardoint` with
   EAX = `'TBL1'`, ESI = XM, ECX = size).

Every synthesized sample is a **rendered 16-step sequencer phrase**: a
one-voice 303-style pattern with notes, ties (glide), accents, a
resonant filter whose cutoff envelope ramps from note to note, and a 3-step
echo that wraps around the phrase. The tracker then arranges these phrases
like loops. Patch "variants" are consecutive slices of one long filter sweep
(§4.4), so the musician automates the "filter" by choosing which variant
sample to trigger.

The original intros were not prepared in any special way: the iXalance
container holds the plain DOS32 executable (unpacked image, BSS as zeros).

## 3. Timing, units, sample format

- **Internal rate.** Synthesis runs at twice the rate of the stored sample and
  is decimated (2:1 in v1, 1/2/4:1 in v2) with **no anti-alias filter**. The
  tracker plays the phrases at note C#5 + relative note, i.e.
  `8363·2^(13/12) = 17 720.6 Hz` per stored sample at 2:1. So the internal rate is
  **R = 35 441.2 Hz**. (This is a design fact deduced from the XM, not a
  constant in the synth.)
- **Step.** One sequencer step is **3530 frames** (loop counter 0..0xDC9
  inclusive) = 99.6 ms at R. That is one tracker row at Jizz/Stash-song-1 tempo
  (speed 6, 151 BPM = 99.3 ms/row). BLUISH transposes the phrases up one
  semitone (D-6) and runs at speed 12 / 160 BPM = 2 steps per row.
- **Phrase.** 16 steps = 56 480 internal frames, written to buffer
  indices **1..56 480** (index 0 is never written by the generator, §4.9).
- **Pitch.** A 16-bit phase accumulator advanced by `pitch` each frame, so
  `f = pitch · R / 65536 = pitch · 0.540 79 Hz`. The data uses a semitone grid over
  ≈ 100: 99/199/399/799 are octaves, and 847, 951, 1007, 1198, 1425 are +1, +3,
  +4, +7, +10 semitones over 799. In absolute terms 799 = 432 Hz (A4 −31 cents);
  the whole song is tuned like that.
- **Output.** Signed 8-bit (the high byte of a clamped 16-bit value),
  delta-coded into the XM. The references write raw (non-delta) signed bytes.

## 4. mini-cauldron v1 (Jizz)

Code 0x4111A2–0x41171C. The working record is at 0x411127–0x4111A1, which is
exactly the 123-byte record. Constants:

| Constant | Address | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C (step divisor) | 0x4110D5 qword | 3529.0 |
| detune ratio | 0x41136E qword | 1.02 |
| accent factor | 0x41159A dword float | 4.0 |
| full scale | 0x411324 qword | 32767.0 |
| glide window | immediate | 1764 (0x6E4) frames |
| step length | immediate | 3530 frames (0..0xDC9) |
| echo stride | immediate | 3529 (0xDC9) |

### 4.1 Patch record (123 bytes)

Offsets are within the record; table at 0x41175A, record *i* at `0x41175A + 123·i`.

| Off | Type | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | f64 | A | filter envelope amount (cutoff coefficient, decays) |
| 0x08 | f64 | B | filter base coefficient (constant during a note) |
| 0x10 | f64 | decay | per-frame multiplier of the envelope part (`v1 *= decay`) |
| 0x18 | f64 | damp | filter damping q (`1 − q` per frame); small = resonant |
| 0x20 | u8 | wave | 0 = saw(s), 2 = spike wave (§4.5), 4 = noise; other = saw |
| 0x21 | u8 | nvar | variants − 1 |
| 0x22 | f64 | mul | per-retrigger multiplier of A and B (compounding, §4.4) |
| 0x2A | f64 | gain | pre-filter gain |
| 0x32 | f64 | echo | echo feedback (§4.8) |
| 0x39 | u8 | pad | unused (low byte of the word that is "pitch[−1]") |
| 0x3A | u8 | voices | 0 = one saw; ≠ 0 = second saw at ×1.02; wave 2 uses the value |
| 0x3B | u16[16] | pitch | per step |
| 0x5B | u8[16] | accent | 1 = accent (envelope and base ×4.0 at retrigger) |
| 0x6B | u8[16] | tie | 1 = tied from previous step: no retrigger, glide, no fade |

The render order is a 12-entry table of 1-based dword record numbers at
0x41172A: `11 12 9 10 6 7 4 8 1 3 5 2`. Record *r*, variant *v* is written to
XM sample `Probe{r+1}{v+1}.smp`. The destination offsets come from a dword
list pointed to by 0x411123 (= 0x40182D), relative to the template at 0x41A6E4.

### 4.2 State

| Address | Type | Name | Reset |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x4110E5 | i16 | phase | 0 at patch start; 0x8002 (−32766) at every retrigger |
| 0x4110E7 | i16 | phase2 | 0 at patch start only |
| 0x411101 | i32 | pitch | retrigger = pitch[s]; glide updates |
| 0x411105 | i32 | out | scratch |
| 0x411109 | i32 | prev | previous filter output; 0 at retrigger |
| 0x4110E9 | f64 | y | filter "velocity" state; 0 at patch start only |
| 0x4110F1 | f64 | v1 | envelope part of the cutoff coefficient |
| 0x4110F9 | f64 | v2 | base part |
| 0x411113 | f64 | q | damp |
| 0x41111B | f64 | d | decay |
| 0x4110E1 | u32 | t | frame counter: incremented before each write; reset to 0 after each render |
| 0x41110F | u16 | s | current step; **survives renders** (16 after a render, 0 initially) |
| 0x41110D / 0x411111 | u16 | n | frame within the step (0..3529) |
| 0x4110DD | ptr | buf | work buffer (zeroed heap; ≥ 58 000 bytes used), **persistent** |

### 4.3 Driver (0x411658)

```text
for each record index r in order[]:                     # 12 patches
    P = copy of record r                                # A and B in the copy get modified
    phase = phase2 = 0; v1 = v2 = q = d = y = 0
    for v in 0 .. nvar:
        retrigger()                                     # uses s left over from last render (16; 0 at first)
        for s in 0..15:
            if tie[s] == 0: retrigger()
            for n in 0..3529: frame(n)
        s = 16
        echo()
        post(r)
```

### 4.4 retrigger (0x411501)

```text
prev  = 0
phase = -32766                     # 0x8002
B = round24(mul * B);  v2 = B      # stored back into P: compounding
A = round24(mul * A);  v1 = A
q = damp;  d = decay
if accent[s] == 1:  v2 *= 4.0;  v1 *= 4.0      # s may be 16: reads tie[0] (always 0 here)
pitch = pitch[s]                                 # s == 16 reads the word formed by accent[0..1]; harmless, step 0 retriggers
```

**The cutoff ramp.** A and B are multiplied by `mul` at every retrigger and
the result is stored back. It is never reset inside a patch. Every new note is
therefore `mul` times brighter (or darker) than the last. The variants continue
the same ramp, so variant *v* is steps `16v..16v+15` of one long sweep. That is
the whole point of `Probe11..14`, `Probe51..58` and so on. Note the double
retrigger at the start of each variant: the variant-start call plus step 0.

### 4.5 frame(n) (0x41132C)

The order of operations matters. "r24" = x87 op rounded to 24 bits (§6).

```text
# 1. oscillator (uses phase BEFORE this frame's increment and pitch from the previous frame)
out = phase
if voices != 0:
    phase2 = i16(phase2 + fistp(r24(pitch * 1.02)))     # second saw, +34 cents
    out += phase2
if wave == 2:  out = spike(...)          # below; overrides out (phase2 clobbered)
elif wave == 4: out = (rng() & 0xFFFFFF) % 64001 - 32000
# 2. pitch: glide on tied steps
if s != 0 and tie[s] == 1:
    if n < 1764:
        a = r24(r24(r24(3529 - 2n) / 3529) * i16(pitch[s-1]))
        b = r24(r24(2n / 3529) * i16(pitch[s]))
        pitch = fistp(r24(a + b))
    else:
        pitch = pitch[s]
# 3. advance
phase = i16(phase + pitch)
# 4. gain + filter
x   = fistp(r24(out * gain))
o   = filter(x)                        # §4.6
t  += 1
prev = o
# 5. per-step amplitude: linear fade over the step when the NEXT step is not tied
nt = (s < 15) ? tie[s+1] : 0xDD        # s == 15 reads the first code byte -> never fades
if nt == 0:  o = fistp(r24(r24(1 - r24(n / 3529)) * o))
o = clamp(o, -32767, 32767)
buf[t] = (o >> 8) & 0xFF               # high byte (floor division, -32767 -> -128)
```

**Spike wave** (wave 2, 0x411264). `V = voices`:

```text
shape(p):  x = p (int16);  if p > 0: x = r24(32767 - p)
           u  = r24(x / 32767)
           u2 = r24(u*u);  u4 = r24(u2*u2);  u6 = r24(u4*u2)
           return r24(r24(u6 * 32767) * u)                  # = 32767·u^7, rounded per op
for k in 0 .. V:                       # QUIRK: every iteration restarts from shape(phase)
    out = fistp(shape(phase))
    if V != 0:
        off = (((t * k) mod 2^32) * (pitch mod 2^32)) >> 7, low 32 bits   # mul / mul / shrd 7
        if k is even: off = -off
        phase2 = i16(phase + off)
        out = fistp(r24(shape(phase2) + out))
# result: shape(phase) + shape(phase ± off(V))
```

`u^7` turns each half of the saw into a narrow spike: for p > 0, u falls 1 → 0;
for p ≤ 0, u rises −1 → 0. Because `off` grows with t, the second voice is
detuned by ±V/128 (+2.3 % for V = 3, negative for even V). The loop discards
voices 1..V−1. Keep that, since it is what the data was tuned to.

### 4.6 filter (0x4111A2)

```text
v1 = r24(v1 * d)                       # envelope decays (or grows: d > 1)
c  = r24(v1 + v2)
y  = r24(y - r24((x + prev) * c))      # x + prev is an exact integer
y  = r24(y * r24(1 - q))
o  = fistp(r24(y + prev))
```

In DSP terms: `y[n] = o[n] − o[n−1]`, so

`o[n] = o[n−1] + (1−q)·( (o[n−1] − o[n−2]) − c·(x[n] + o[n−1]) )`

This is a **damped mass–spring (2-pole resonant low-pass)** that pulls the
output towards **−x** (DC gain −1). The natural frequency is
`ω ≈ √c rad/frame`, so `fc ≈ √c · R / 2π` (c = 0.005 → 399 Hz, 0.1 → 1.78 kHz),
and the resonance is `Q ≈ √c / q`. `c = v1 + v2` is the cutoff
coefficient: base v2 plus an exponentially decaying envelope v1. It is a
303-style envelope-modulated resonant low-pass.

### 4.7 Sequencer semantics

| Step type | Effect |
|---|---|
| `tie[s] = 0` | retrigger: phase reset to −32766, filter-drive memory `prev` = 0 (y kept), envelope restarts at the compounded A/B, accent applies |
| `tie[s] = 1` | no retrigger; pitch glides linearly from pitch[s−1] to pitch[s] over the first 1764 frames (half a step) |
| fade | the step fades linearly to 0 across its 3530 frames unless the next step is tied (legato) |
| accent | ×4 on both envelope amount and base at that retrigger (v2: ×2) |

### 4.8 echo (0x4111ED), over the whole phrase, in place

```text
for e in 0..15, k in 0..3529:          # address e*3529 + k: one frame overlaps per step
    i = e*3529 + k
    src = (e >= 3) ? buf[i - 3*3529] : buf[i + 13*3529]
    v = int8(buf[i]) + fistp(r24(int8(src) * echo))
    buf[i] = clamp(v, -127, 127)
```

The delay is 3 steps (≈ 0.3 s), with feedback (sources at e ≥ 3 are already
echoed). The first three steps take the **un-echoed** tail of steps 13–15, so
the phrase loops without a gap. Index 0 is touched (e = 0, k = 0).

### 4.9 post (0x4115DC) and output

```text
if r == 7: buf[0..57999] = int8(buf) >> 1           # hard-coded: record 7 halved
for i in 0..28000: buf[i] = buf[2i]                 # 2:1 decimation, in place, no filter
t = 0
sample = buf[0..27999]                              # 28 000 bytes: the last 240 output frames of step 15 are cut
delta-code into the XM at offsets[k++]              # the buffer is delta-coded in place too
```

`buf[0]` is never generated. It is only modified by the echo
(`buf[0] += round(buf[45877]·echo)`), survives every render, and is decimated
into output sample 0. Bit-exact output therefore needs the persistent buffer
(initially all zero) and the exact render order.

## 5. mini-cauldron v2 (Stash)

Code 0x402A31–0x403178, working record 0x401D1C–0x401D82 (103 bytes). The
same design with these changes.

|---|---|---|
| C | 0x401998 qword | 3529.0 |
| detune | 0x402C1D qword | 1.02 |

### 5.1 Record (103 bytes, float32 parameters)

| Off | Type | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | f32 | B | base coefficient |
| 0x04 | f32 | damp | q |
| 0x08 | f32 | A | envelope amount |

| 0x14 | f32 | gain | pre-filter gain |
| 0x18 | f32 | echo | echo feedback |
| 0x1C | u8 | wave | 0 saw · 1 square · 2 spike' · 3 pulse · 4 noise · 5 fold (§5.3) |
| 0x1D | u8 | nvar | variants − 1 |
| 0x1E | u8 | decim | 1 → 59 000 bytes, 2 → 29 000, else → 14 000; read step = decim |

| 0x20 | u8 | doubler | ≠ 0: run the doubler (§5.5) |
| 0x21 | u8 | — | unused |
| 0x22 | u8 | comb | ≠ 0: comb distance k (§5.5) |

### 5.2 Driver (0x40302D, self-modifying, called twice)

```text
call 0: table 0x4037ED (10 records), 11 slots, drum count 6, flag 0   -> XM at 0x5389B8 "BLUISH" (played second)
then the code patches itself: drum count 7 (imm at 0x40304B), slots 0x11 (imm at 0x403140),
     table 0x40317D (imm at 0x4030B7), flag byte 0x402F0F = 1
call 1: table 0x40317D (16 records), 17 slots, drum count 7, flag 1   -> XM at 0x45CF38 (untitled, played first)

for slot in 0 .. slots-1:
    if slot == 1: copy the stored drum kit (drum count samples) to the next sample slots; continue
    P = copy of record (slot == 0 ? 0 : slot-1)
    phase = phase2 = 0; v1 = v2 = q = d = y = 0
    for v in 0..nvar:
        retrigger(); segments()                    # as v1
        if comb: comb(comb)
        echo()                                     # as v1 (f32 feedback)
        if doubler: doubler()
        post(slot, flag)
```

The XM sample slots are consumed in XM order: every non-empty sample, the
drum slot taking `drum count` of them. The work buffer (heap, zeroed at
start) persists across renders **and across the two calls**. The RNG is
(0, 0, 0) and unused, because Stash has no noise patches.

**Drum kit.** 7 stored samples, delta-coded bytes copied verbatim, lengths
2950, 1128, 1284, 4104, 1456, 3419 (`Ohh.smp`), 313. The length table is at
0x403BF7 + 8·i and the data (14 654 bytes) at 0x408C7C. Call 0 copies 6; call 1
copies 7 (the 313-byte forward loop is song 1's instrument 10). They are not
synthesized.

### 5.3 frame(n) (0x402BDB)

```text
out = phase
if voices: phase2 = i16(phase2 + fistp(r24(i32(pitch) * 1.02))); out += phase2
switch wave:
  1: out = (out > 0) ? 30000 : -30000                  # square; with voices = 2-saw-sum sign = PWM
  2: out = spike2()                                    # below
  3: out = (out > 31000) ? 30000 : 0                   # narrow pulse
  4: out = (rng() & 0xFFFFFF) % 64001 - 32000          # RNG at 0x44B64B, same algorithm as v1
  5: if out > 0: out = 32767 - out                     # fold
out = clamp(out, -32767, 32767)                        # NEW: clamp before the filter
glide (as v1, pitch[] read as signed int16 in fimul)
phase = i16(phase + pitch)
t += 1                                                 # t now increments BEFORE the filter
x = fistp(r24(out * gain));  o = filter(x);  prev = o
if tie[s+1] == 0:                                      # gate instead of the whole-step fade
    if i16(n) > i16(hold):                             # signed 16-bit compare
        if n > (hold + release) & 0xFFFF: o = 0
        else: o = fistp(r24(r24(1 - r24(r24(n - i16(hold)) / i16(release))) * o))
o = clamp(o, -32767, 32767);  buf[t] = (o >> 8) & 0xFF
```

**spike2** differs from v1 in three ways:
- The base voice has **no sign branch**: `u = (32767 − p)/32767 ∈ (0, 2)`, so
  the negative half of the saw explodes (u^7 up to 128) and is clipped by the
  clamp. The result is a half-clipped square-like spike.
- `off = (((t·k) & 0xFFFF) · pitch) >> 7`: the 16-bit `mul bx` truncation
  makes the detuned voice jump when `t·k` wraps.
- There is no negation.

The same "only the last voice survives" loop quirk applies.

### 5.4 retrigger (0x402E34)

As in v1, with f32 parameters (`fld dword`, products rounded to 24 bits and
stored back with `fst dword`) and accent ×2.0.

### 5.5 Post chain

| Stage | Address | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| comb | 0x402A7C | `for i in 0 .. 59000-k-1: buf[i] = clamp(int8(buf[i+k]) − int8(buf[i]), −126, 127)` in place. A feedforward difference comb: notches every R/k; k = 4 is a high-pass-ish edge, k = 150 a 236 Hz comb. Reads stale bytes above 56 480. |
| echo | 0x402AAF | as v1, `fmul dword` feedback |
| doubler | 0x402FF0 | `for i in 2..58000: j = (i·0xFF3B + 0x8000) >> 16 (32-bit); v = (int8(buf[j]) + int8(buf[i])) >> 1; if j−1 < 58000: buf[j−1] = v`. Mixes in a copy running at 0.99699× in place: a slowly growing delay (0 → ≈ 175 frames), i.e. a flange/chorus sweep over the phrase. |
| specials (flag = 1 only) | 0x402F10 | slot 9: `buf[i] += int8(buf[i]) >> 2` for 60 000 bytes (×1.25 **with 8-bit wrap-around**); slots 11 and 15: `buf[i] += 0x30` (+48 **with wrap**). Hard-coded, deliberate distortion. |
| decimate | | decim 1: 59 000 bytes as-is; 2: 29 000 bytes `buf[i] = buf[2i]`; other (4 in the data): 14 000 bytes `buf[i] = buf[i·decim]` |
| tail | | bytes `len−19 .. len−5`: `(int8·(15..1)) >> 4` (low byte); bytes `len−4..len−1` = 0 |
| output | | t = 0; copy `len` bytes; the buffer is **delta-coded in place** (the next render's comb, doubler and specials read those stale delta bytes above the phrase) |

Note: 59 000 > 56 480 and 29 000 > 28 240, so decim-1 and decim-2 samples
carry about 2.5 k / 760 bytes of stale buffer after the phrase. The tail fade
masks the end. A bit-exact port must reproduce the stale data.

## 6. Numeric model (needed for bit-exact output)

- The intro runs `fninit` and then clears the precision-control bits
  (0x413825–0x41383E in Jizz): **FPU CW = 0x007F**, i.e. 24-bit significand,
  round-to-nearest-even, all exceptions masked.
- Every x87 add/sub/mul/div result is rounded **once** to a 24-bit significand.
  The exponent keeps the extended range, but that never matters with this data.
  Loads (`fld qword`, `fild`) and qword stores of already-rounded values are
  exact. `fistp` rounds to nearest-even.
- Several operands are full f64 constants (record values in v1, 1.02, 3529).
  Computing in f32 would round them first, and computing in f64 would double-round.
  The reference (`ref/x87pc24.py`) computes the exact result as `hi + lo`
  (TwoSum / FMA TwoProduct) and rounds that to 24 bits. Division through f64 then
  24-bit rounding is exact because 53 ≥ 2·24 + 2.
- Sensitivity: with plain f64 arithmetic, between 2 and 1111 bytes per
  28 000-byte Jizz sample differ by ±1 LSB. That is inaudible, but not bit-exact.

**Rust recipe.** Keep every value in `f64`. Write `fn r24(hi: f64, lo: f64) -> f64`:
round hi's 53-bit significand to 24 bits with ties-to-even, using `lo`
to break exact midpoints. Use `f64::mul_add(a, b, -p)` for the product error
and TwoSum for add/sub. `fistp` is `round_ties_even()`.

## 7. RNG (v1 0x40F640, v2 0x44B64B)

Three 32-bit words s0, s1, s2. It is shared with the graphics code.

```text
a = s0 + 0x0E372471;  b = s1 + 0x00BC5EA9;  c = s2 + 0x00C8ADE7
a ^= b
(hi:lo) = signed(a) * signed(c);  a = lo;  d = rol(hi, 13) + a - b
c = rol(c, 14);  a ^= d;  b += c;  c ^= d;  a -= b;  b = rol(b, 9)
(hi:lo) = a * c (unsigned);  a = lo;  b ^= hi
a += 0x01E42AC3;  b += 0x005EDF04;  c += 0x011A2EC1
s0, s1, s2 = b, c, a;  return a
```

The state starts at (0, 0, 0). In Jizz, 80 draws happen before the synth
(state 0x86684929, 0x6525C79E, 0xB3EECD3E), and only record 10 (`Probe111`,
the first render) uses noise.

## 9. Implementation notes for a port

- **Scope.** The synth is a function `render(patch, variant state) -> i8[]`
  with persistent state across renders: compounded A/B inside a patch, `buf`,
  step register, RNG. Port v1 and v2 as two code paths or one parameterised
  engine. v2 is a superset except for these v1-only rules: no clamp before the
  filter, the t-increment position, the whole-step fade, accent 4.0, f64 record
  fields, the halving of record 7, and the spike-wave sign handling.
- **Golden tests.** `ref/golden_sha256.json` has SHA-256 hashes of every raw
  render (`render_jizz/<Probe…>`, `render_stash/<song>_<slot>_<variant>`) as
  produced by the references. A Rust port can assert those hashes from the
  JSON patch data alone, with no copyrighted audio checked in. Render in the
  same order (Jizz: `order`; Stash: call 0 then call 1) with one persistent
  buffer.
- **Cost.** 56 480 frames × ≈ 40 ops per render; all 85 renders take
  milliseconds in Rust. The Python reference needs about 30 s per intro.
- **Size (for our 64k).** v1 code is ≈ 1.4 KB of x87, plus 123 B per patch and an
  ≈ 15 KB xz template. A Rust port of just the generator plus the XM side is
  feasible as a precalc stage. In `unisynth` terms, a generated buffer is a
  sampler-slot source, rendered at load. The phrases are fixed by the patch,
  so a song would have to be authored around 16-step phrase samples.
- **Port target (decided): sound-faithful, not bit-exact.** The 8-bit
  storage tricks existed to fit ~1 MB of samples into GUS memory, not to
  shape the sound. Drop them:
  - plain f32/f64 math instead of x87 PC24 (±1 LSB, §6);
  - keep the voice/echo/comb/doubler path in float, clamping only at the
    final output, instead of 8-bit storage between stages;
  - no 2:1/4:1 decimation (that halved memory and added aliasing); render at
    the player rate;
  - no delta coding, no stale tail bytes, no persistent `buf[0]`;
  - no `rec 7` halving: fold it into that patch's gain.
  Keep what is the synth itself: the 16-bit phase wrap (it *is* the saw),
  the compounding `mul` cutoff ramp across notes and variants, the echo
  wrap-around, the gate/fade and glide timing, the clamp before the v2 filter
  (it shapes spike2), and the "only the last spike voice" behaviour (the
  patches were tuned to it).
  The Stash flag-1 specials (slot 9 ×1.25, slots 11/15 +48, both with 8-bit
  wrap): only slot 9's wraps in the rendered samples (135 jumps); 11 and 15
  stay under 80, so their +48 is a DC shift. The port keeps slot 9's as a wrap
  effect and drops the +48.
- **The port as built:** `synth-convert xm` (change add-cauldron-xm-import)
  plays the phrases live in `unisynth`; see "Mini-cauldron XMs" in
  `docs/synth/unisynth.md`.
